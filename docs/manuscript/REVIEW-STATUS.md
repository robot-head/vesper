## Operational proof coverage

The one-binding, recursion-free `MetatheoryAppendix` fragment now proves evaluation existence, result preservation, per-resource identity conservation, and fresh IDs with exactly-once consumption for closed copy-result programs (`closed_exact_once`). Evaluation is a conclusion rather than an assumed premise. The canonical-value relation rejects a scalar masquerading as a linear handle, which previously allowed a stuck `consume(var)` despite an allegedly typed environment. This proof still covers only the fragment's variables, literals, mint, consume, fail, and let operations; it does not cover the surface language's higher-order functions, ADTs, heap, borrowing, handlers, recursion, or ghost erasure.

The conservative annotated extension in `lean/GhostErasure.lean` proves bidirectional big-step erasure equivalence and ghost-environment non-interference while preserving runtime resource traces. Closed ghost resource computations must terminate and discharge their own tokens before erasure; rejected examples cover leaks, failures, and runtime observation of ghost values. This does not implement ghost syntax in the compiler or prove small-step bisimulation for the whole language.

`lean/OperationalGhostBridge.lean` now connects closed, terminating ghost resource proofs to the actual polymorphic runtime calculus. It proves trace equivalence, progress/preservation, no infinite ghost-only stuttering, and per-kind runtime resource exhaustion, including worked recursion and stateful-handler traces. This closes a limited composition gap; its instrumentation is not a source elaboration, and captured ghost tokens and dependent heap typing remain outside it.

The larger existing calculi have now also been checked: `MetatheoryFull`, `Metatheory/Closures`, `Metatheory/Polymorphism`, and `MetatheoryPlaces`. Their progress/preservation and resource proofs cover closures, stateful handlers, polymorphism, recursion safety, and mutable borrowing in their respective models. The first three contained the same meet-absorption defect; they now join field floors, carry the field well-formedness inequalities through value preservation, and reject scalar linear wrappers at copy mode. Generic example types now use their actual field bounds rather than the former neutral `linear` cap. These calculi are separate, not one proof of all features together or a proof of the native runtime.

`lean/PlacePolymorphismBridge.lean` connects initialized place values, typed paths, and frame updates to the actual polymorphic calculus. It preserves kinds and exact ownership, constructs the exchange typing derivation, and invokes the existing preservation theorem for exchange both directly and under evaluation contexts. The displaced owner is returned. Checked examples reject duplicate handles and uninitialized inputs. This does not merge heap-stored closures, captured borrows, allocation, or source elaboration into either calculus.

The proof-admission boundary now rejects compiler-trust axioms consistently in extraction, standalone auditing, and publication, including Lean 4.35's generated `_native` axioms. Extraction cache hits are compared against a fresh compilation before replay, closing substitution of a valid unrelated artifact under the current source key. Chapter 8 now describes these actual gates and removes its permissive illustrative audit implementation. Explicit extraction contract requests now require complete coverage and exact replayed signatures with no extra hypotheses. The gate rejects unrelated proofs and hidden section-instance assumptions. Explicit contract admission now also requires an exact closed proof of the generated existential input precondition for every contract and permits only standard Lean axioms. This closes the contradictory-precondition admission gap. Termination, extraction correctness, publication integration, and native executable correspondence still require separate justification.

`lean/CodegenComposition.lean` composes the existing direct-backend instruction-selection theorem over finite straight-line row streams. It preserves source/target results and aborts and excludes abstract hardware faults under explicit reachable-row operand preconditions. Worked examples cover normal addition, overflow short-circuiting, and rejection of an ill-typed continuation. `lean/CodegenControlFlow.lean` extends this to abstract branches, jumps, halts, finite prefixes, and preservation/reflection of divergence under an explicit inductive invariant. It interprets control instructions in the actual selected blocks and includes a counterexample showing why Boolean operand bounds cannot be dropped. These models do not prove Rust lowering, discharge the invariant for compiler output, or establish calls, frame layout, physical control addresses, byte encodings, or hardware semantics.

`lean/CodegenValidation.lean` adds an executable conservative validator for finite data-row streams. Acceptance establishes the straight-line simulation's operand conditions for every store satisfying its input facts, including programs with no input assumptions. The transfer proof covers aliased destinations and frame-area copies, while regressions reject stale facts and unknown/unrepresentable operands. Successful remainder results retain their proved kind ranges, including the signed-minimum/minus-one boundary. `lean/CodegenFlowValidation.lean` checks finite per-row certificates across branches, jumps, and back edges and proves that acceptance establishes the control-flow invariant. It derives prefix simulation, hardware-fault exclusion, and divergence equivalence, with checked loop/branch results and rejection of forged ranges and invalid edges. Missing labels still produce the modeled unreachable abort. The flow checker also lets a constant claim any kind containing its value, gives aborts no successor, and `widened_validation_sound` transfers acceptance of comparison-widened rows (same selected instructions and source steps) to the original rows. `src/verified_codegen/certificate.rs` generates certificates for compiler output by an interprocedural range analysis and validates them with a Rust mirror of `validateProgram` that is differentially tested against Lean `#eval` on accepted and mutated certificates; every verified build records the outcome, and `Options::require_certificate` refuses rejected programs (off by default until facts are variant-sensitive for enum payload words and `Fail` status words, which the milestone 7 fixtures need, and cover continuations of non-returning calls). Its rules for `Enter`, `Call` (entry and exit summaries over the incoming area), `Return` and trap-scope handler edges (a caught failure must establish the handler's annotation from the failing row's facts) are conservative but not proved in Lean, and the mirror itself is tested, not proved; these checks do not prove termination, absence of all language aborts, calls, physical addresses, or native byte encodings.
